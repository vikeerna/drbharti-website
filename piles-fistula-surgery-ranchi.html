Understanding the condition

Piles, fissure or fistula: how they differ

Piles (haemorrhoids, बवासीर) are swollen blood vessels in and around the anus. An anal fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anus. An anal fistula (भगंदर) is a small tunnel between the inside of the anus and the skin, usually after an abscess. They feel different and are treated differently, so an examination matters.

Common symptoms

  • Piles: bright red bleeding during stool, a soft lump at the anus, itching; often little pain
  • Fissure: sharp, cutting pain during and after passing stool, sometimes with a streak of blood
  • Fistula: repeated boils, pus or discharge near the anus, with pain or swelling
  • Pilonidal sinus: swelling, pain or discharge near the top of the buttock cleft

Rectal bleeding should not be assumed to be piles. Bleeding with weight loss, change in bowel habit or a family history of bowel cancer needs proper examination.

Piles (haemorrhoids)

Piles treatment in Ranchi

Piles are swollen blood vessels in and around the anus. They are very common, yet many people delay seeing a doctor out of embarrassment. A consultation is private and examination is done with care.

Early piles often settle with fibre, fluids and medicines. For larger (grade 3–4) piles, or piles that have not improved with other treatment, Dr. Bharti performs open haemorrhoidectomy, the surgical removal of the piles. Recovery usually takes two to four weeks with wound care.

  • Bright red bleeding during or after passing stool
  • Pain, burning or itching around the anus
  • A lump coming out of the anus that may need to be pushed back
  • A feeling of incomplete emptying

Anal fistula

Fistula treatment (fistulectomy)

An anal fistula is a small tunnel between the anal canal and the skin, usually after an abscess. It causes repeated discharge, itching and infections and rarely heals on its own.

Dr. Bharti performs fistulectomy, removal of the fistula tract, and other sphincter-preserving techniques such as a seton or an advancement flap when the tract involves more muscle. Protecting bowel control is a priority in every fistula operation.

Anal fissure

Fissure treatment (lateral internal sphincterotomy)

A chronic anal fissure is a tear in the anal lining that causes sharp pain during and after passing stool, often with a little bleeding. A tight anal muscle stops it from healing.

Many fissures heal with stool softeners and prescribed ointments. When they do not, lateral internal sphincterotomy (LIS) relaxes the muscle so that the fissure can heal.

Medical names, explained simply

Conditions and operations you may hear about

Haemorrhoidectomy for piles

An operation to remove troublesome haemorrhoids. The grade and symptoms of the piles guide whether this is appropriate.

Sphincterotomy for anal fissure

A selected portion of the internal anal muscle is divided to reduce tightness and help a chronic fissure heal. The risk to bowel control needs an individual discussion.

Fistulotomy, fistulectomy and staged care

Fistulotomy opens the tract; fistulectomy removes it. A seton or a sphincter-preserving approach may be discussed for fistulas involving more muscle. An advancement flap covers the inner opening with nearby tissue.

Rectal prolapse and laparoscopic rectopexy

Rectal prolapse is when the rectum slips down and can protrude outside. Rectopexy supports it from inside the abdomen; it is different from piles surgery.

Pilonidal sinus

A tract near the top of the buttock cleft can cause swelling or discharge. Assessment determines whether drainage or planned sinus surgery is needed.

The procedure is chosen after assessment. A technique listed here is not suitable for every patient.

An individual treatment plan

Options to discuss

Piles

Bowel-habit measures and medicines can help some patients; persistent or more advanced disease may need a procedure.

Anal fissure

A tear in the anal lining may improve with stool-softening measures and prescribed treatment. An operation may be considered when appropriate.

Anal fistula

A tunnel near the anus often needs surgery. Its relationship to the muscles controlling stool affects the treatment choice.

These are general options, not a recommendation for an individual patient. Your examination and reports guide the advice.

Planning ahead

Recovery and return to routine

Recovery and wound care differ considerably between procedures. Discuss pain relief, bowel movements, dressings, time away from work and follow-up before surgery.

  • Hospital stayPlanned piles, fissure or fistula surgery usually means going home the next day.
  • PainPain is managed with medicines; warm sitz baths help.
  • DietA fibre-rich diet and plenty of water keep stools soft and help healing.
  • WorkDesk work after one to two weeks; full recovery in three to four weeks.

Bleeding during bowel movements — what to do?

Bright red bleeding during or after stool is often caused by piles or a fissure, but other conditions can cause it, so do not self-treat for long. See a surgeon for examination, especially if there is weight loss, a change in bowel habit or a family history of bowel cancer.

Is piles surgery painful?

The operation is done under anaesthesia, so you do not feel pain during it. Pain afterwards is expected and is managed with medicines, sitz baths and soft stools.

Can a fistula heal without surgery?

An anal fistula rarely heals on its own and usually needs surgery. The operation is planned to remove or open the tract while protecting the muscles that control bowel movements.

What is the difference between piles and fistula?

Piles are swollen blood vessels that cause bleeding or a lump. A fistula is a small tunnel from inside the anal canal to the skin, causing discharge and repeated boils. They are different conditions with different treatments.

How long is recovery after piles surgery?

Most planned cases go home the next day. Desk work is usually possible after one to two weeks and full recovery takes three to four weeks. A high-fibre diet and plenty of water help healing.

Which doctor should I see for piles, fissure or fistula in Ranchi?

These conditions are treated by a general surgeon. Many early cases settle with diet and medicines, so an operation is not always the first step. Is laser treatment better for piles or fistula?

Laser is one technique for selected cases. It is not suitable for every patient, and recurrence can occur with any method. Ask which technique is proposed for your condition and why.

Can piles be treated without surgery?

Yes, many patients improve with fibre, fluids, stool softeners and prescribed medicines. Persistent bleeding, prolapsing piles or other features may need a procedure after examination.

Can I bring a recommendation from another hospital?

Yes. Bring the treatment recommendation and available reports for an in-person second opinion.

When to seek urgent care

Heavy or ongoing bleeding, faintness, or a painful swelling with fever needs urgent medical assessment.
