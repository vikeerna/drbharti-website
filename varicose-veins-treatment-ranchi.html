Understanding the condition

What are varicose veins?

Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins under the skin, usually in the legs. They develop when valves inside the veins stop working properly, so blood pools in the leg. Standing for long hours, pregnancy, family history and increasing age make them more likely.

Varicose vein symptoms

  • Visible bulging, blue or purple veins on the legs
  • Aching, heavy or tired legs, worse after standing
  • Swelling around the ankles, itching or night cramps
  • Darkening or hardening of the skin near the ankle, eczema or a slow-healing wound

A bleeding vein needs urgent care. Sudden painful swelling of one leg, chest pain or breathlessness needs emergency assessment.

Why they form

Why varicose veins develop: valve failure explained

Leg veins carry blood upward to the heart against gravity. Small one-way valves inside them stop the blood from falling back down.

When these valves weaken, blood leaks backward and pools in the lower leg. This is called venous reflux. The raised pressure stretches the veins, so they bulge and twist, and pushes fluid and pigment into the skin. Because damaged valves do not repair themselves, varicose veins tend to slowly worsen unless the faulty vein is treated.

  • Bulging veins that become more prominent over time
  • Brown discolouration around the ankle
  • Pain, heaviness and swelling after standing
  • Itching, eczema and, in advanced cases, ulcers

The procedure

How radiofrequency ablation (RFA) is done, step by step

Dr. Bharti performs RFA at Samford Super Speciality Hospital in the operation theatre, under ultrasound guidance and local anaesthesia. RFA is a modern alternative to open vein stripping and is comparable to laser (EVLT) treatment.

  • Ultrasound mapping: the faulty vein is located and marked
  • Needle puncture and sheath: the vein is entered through a needle puncture instead of a surgical cut
  • Catheter placement: a fine RFA catheter is passed along the vein
  • Controlled heating: radiofrequency energy seals the vein from inside, segment by segment; blood flow continues through healthy deep veins
  • Time and stay: the treatment usually takes 45–60 minutes, and patients usually go home the next day

Medical names, explained simply

Conditions and operations you may hear about

Varicose veins and venous reflux

Varicose veins are enlarged surface veins. Venous reflux means blood flows backwards through faulty valves. Duplex ultrasound helps identify the veins involved; all leg swelling is not caused by varicose veins.

Radiofrequency ablation (RFA)

A thin catheter delivers controlled heat inside a faulty vein to close it. It is a vein treatment, not removal of the entire network of leg veins.

Vein ligation and stripping

Surgical tying off and removal of a faulty vein are other approaches in Dr. Bharti’s operative experience. The operation may include treatment at the junction where a surface vein joins a deeper vein. The assessment determines which approach is appropriate.

The procedure is chosen after assessment. A technique listed here is not suitable for every patient.

Your questions

Before you decide

Write down anything you are unsure about and bring it to your consultation.

Swollen, twisted veins on the legs — what is the treatment in Ranchi?

These are usually varicose veins caused by leaking valves. After examination and a venous Doppler scan, treatment may include compression, radiofrequency ablation (RFA), foam sclerotherapy or surgery. Dr. Vikeerna Bharti performs RFA at Samford Super Speciality Hospital, Ranchi. Call 62060 91982.

Is varicose vein RFA treatment painful?

RFA is done under local anaesthesia. Most patients feel the anaesthetic injections and a mild warmth or pressure; some soreness or bruising is common for a few days afterwards.

Is RFA done without a surgical cut?

RFA is usually performed through a small puncture or access opening and a thin sheath; additional vein procedures may need small incisions. The puncture is covered with a small dressing.

Will there be a scar after varicose vein treatment?

RFA leaves only small puncture marks, which usually fade over time. Open vein surgery leaves small scars at the incision sites.

Can I go home the same day after varicose vein treatment?

After RFA at Samford, patients usually go home the next day. You can walk on the day of treatment.

Do I need to wear compression stockings after RFA?

Many patients wear stockings for a short period after treatment. Whether and how long you need them depends on your treatment and findings; your surgeon will advise you.

What is the cost of varicose vein treatment in Ranchi?

The cost depends on which veins are affected, one or both legs, the procedure and consumables, and the hospital stay. An estimate is given after examination and a venous Doppler scan. Cashless treatment is available at Samford under major health-insurance and TPA plans, Ayushman Bharat and the Jharkhand Rajya Karmi Swasthya Bima Yojana; the hospital insurance desk confirms eligibility.

Can varicose veins come back after RFA?

The treated vein usually stays closed, but new varicose veins can develop over the years, and some patients need further treatment. Staying active, keeping a healthy weight and attending follow-up help.

Are varicose veins dangerous if left untreated?

Many varicose veins cause only symptoms, but they can progress to skin darkening, swelling, eczema, ulcers or bleeding, and sometimes to blood clots. Assessment helps decide whether and when treatment is needed.
