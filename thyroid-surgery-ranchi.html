Understanding the condition

What are thyroid swellings?

The thyroid is a butterfly-shaped gland at the front of the neck. A goitre (घेंघा) is an enlarged gland; a nodule is a lump within it. Most nodules are not cancer, but they need assessment with blood tests, ultrasound and sometimes a needle test (FNAC).

Thyroid swelling symptoms

  • A swelling at the front of the neck that moves when you swallow
  • A feeling of pressure, difficulty swallowing or breathing when lying flat
  • A change in voice or hoarseness
  • Symptoms of an over- or under-active thyroid, such as weight change, palpitations or tiredness

Rapid growth of a lump, voice change or breathing difficulty needs prompt assessment.

When surgery is considered

Thyroid conditions that may need an operation

Many thyroid problems are treated with medicines or only need monitoring. Surgery is considered for some conditions.

  • Goitre (enlarged thyroid) causing pressure, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or a visible swelling
  • Thyroid nodules that are large, growing, or need removal for diagnosis
  • Multinodular goitre: several nodules enlarging the gland
  • Over-active thyroid (for example Graves disease) not controlled by medicines

The operation

Types of thyroid surgery

Dr. Bharti performs hemithyroidectomy and total thyroidectomy for benign thyroid disease. He does not operate on thyroid cancer; if tests suggest cancer, he will explain the findings and refer you to a specialist cancer centre.

  • Hemithyroidectomy (lobectomy): removal of one half of the gland
  • Total thyroidectomy: removal of the whole gland, followed by lifelong thyroid hormone tablets
  • Hospital stay is usually one to two days

Start with a careful assessment

A thyroid swelling or nodule does not automatically mean cancer or a need for surgery. Assessment can include thyroid blood tests, ultrasound and a needle sample when indicated.

Medical names, explained simply

Conditions and operations you may hear about

Thyroid nodule and goitre

A nodule is a lump within the thyroid; goitre means enlargement of the gland. Tests guide whether observation, medicine or surgery is advised.

Hemithyroidectomy

Removal of one half of the thyroid; also called thyroid lobectomy.

Total thyroidectomy

Removal of the whole thyroid. Hormone replacement and follow-up are part of care, not an optional addition.

The procedure is chosen after assessment. A technique listed here is not suitable for every patient.

An individual treatment plan

Options to discuss

Observation or medical treatment

Some conditions can be monitored or treated without an operation.

Removing one side

Hemithyroidectomy removes one lobe of the thyroid. The reason for surgery determines whether this is appropriate.

Removing the whole thyroid

Total thyroidectomy removes the gland and requires ongoing thyroid hormone replacement. Discuss why that extent of surgery is proposed.

These are general options, not a recommendation for an individual patient. Your examination and reports guide the advice.

Recovery and return to routine

Recovery includes wound care and review of the final tissue report. Thyroid hormone and calcium checks or treatment may be needed; follow the individual discharge plan.

I have a swelling in my neck — do I need surgery?

A swelling at the front of the neck is most often a goitre or thyroid nodule, and many do not need surgery. Assessment includes examination, thyroid blood tests, ultrasound and, when needed, an FNAC needle test. Surgery is discussed for large, growing or pressure-causing swellings and some over-active thyroid conditions.

Is thyroid surgery risky?

Thyroidectomy is a well-established operation. Its specific risks include bleeding in the neck, voice change from nerve irritation and low calcium from effects on the parathyroid glands; most effects are temporary. These are explained before surgery.

Will I need thyroid tablets after surgery?

After total thyroidectomy, lifelong thyroid hormone (thyroxine) tablets are needed, usually once a day. After hemithyroidectomy, many patients do not need tablets; this is checked with blood tests at follow-up.

What is the recovery time after thyroid surgery?

Most patients go home in one to two days and return to normal activities in one to two weeks. The voice may be slightly hoarse for a few days.

Does Dr. Bharti operate on thyroid cancer?

No. Dr. Bharti operates on benign thyroid conditions such as goitre and benign nodules. If tests suggest thyroid cancer, he will explain the reports and refer you to a specialist cancer centre.

Does every thyroid nodule need surgery?

No. Many nodules only need monitoring. Surgery is discussed for suspicious test results, large or growing swellings, pressure symptoms or some over-active thyroid conditions.

Will I need tablets after thyroid surgery?

After total thyroidectomy, thyroid hormone tablets are needed for life. After removal of half the gland, some patients still need tablets; this is checked at follow-up.

Can I bring a recommendation from another hospital?

Yes. Bring the treatment recommendation and available reports for an in-person second opinion.

When to seek urgent care

Rapid neck swelling or difficulty breathing requires emergency care, particularly after thyroid surgery.
