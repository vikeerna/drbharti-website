Understanding the condition

What are gallbladder stones?

The gallbladder is a small pouch under the liver that stores bile. Gallbladder stones (gallstones, पित्त की पथरी) form when bile hardens into stones. Many people have no symptoms; others have repeated attacks of pain or complications such as inflammation of the gallbladder, jaundice or pancreatitis.

Gallbladder stone symptoms: where is gallbladder pain felt?

  • Pain in the upper right side of the abdomen, or the upper middle part just below the ribs
  • Pain that may spread to the back or the right shoulder blade
  • Attacks that often start after a meal, especially oily food, and can last from about half an hour to several hours
  • Nausea, vomiting, bloating or indigestion with the pain
  • The location of the pain is the same in women and men; gallstones are more common in women

Acidity, ulcers, kidney stones and heart problems can cause similar pain, so an examination and ultrasound are needed before deciding on treatment. Fever, yellow eyes or constant severe pain need urgent hospital care.

Gallbladder surgery explained in a diagram

The standard treatment

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy

For gallstones that cause symptoms, removal of the gallbladder (cholecystectomy) is the recommended treatment, and today it is usually done by the laparoscopic (keyhole) method.

Dr. Bharti makes three or four small cuts of 5–10 mm. A camera and fine instruments are passed through them, the gallbladder is separated from the liver and bile ducts, and it is removed through one of the small cuts. Open surgery is used when the findings make keyhole surgery unsafe.

  • Is life normal without a gallbladder? Yes. The liver keeps making bile, which then flows directly into the intestine. Most people have no long-term restriction on diet after recovery.

The first consultation

Start with your symptoms and scan

Gallstones may cause no symptoms, or cause episodes of pain in the upper abdomen, sometimes with nausea. Symptoms alone do not establish the diagnosis. Assessment may include an ultrasound and blood tests.

Conditions and operations you may hear about

Gallstones and gallbladder inflammation

Gallstones are also called cholelithiasis. Inflammation of the gallbladder is called cholecystitis; infected fluid in the gallbladder is called empyema. These findings can change the urgency and complexity of treatment.

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy

Keyhole removal of the gallbladder. Dr. Bharti’s experience includes difficult gallbladder surgery, including severe scarring and Mirizzi syndrome, where a stone can compress the nearby bile duct.

Common bile duct stones and exploration

Choledocholithiasis means stones in the main bile duct. Depending on the findings, treatment may involve ERCP by an endoscopy team or surgical bile duct exploration. These are different from removing stones inside the gallbladder.

An individual treatment plan

Options to discuss

Is surgery always necessary?

Stones found incidentally without symptoms may not require treatment. Symptoms, complications and stones in the bile duct change the decision.

Laparoscopic gallbladder removal

When advised, cholecystectomy removes the gallbladder containing the stones through small incisions. Ask how the proposed operation fits your findings.

What if the bile duct contains stones?

Bile-duct stones may need a separate procedure such as ERCP. Your assessment determines the treatment sequence and whether additional care is needed.

These are general options, not a recommendation for an individual patient. Your examination and reports guide the advice.

Planning ahead

Recovery and return to routine

After uncomplicated keyhole surgery, discharge may be on the same day or after an overnight stay. Return to work often takes around one to two weeks, depending on your recovery and the work you do. Open surgery or complications can require longer recovery. Follow your own discharge advice about wounds, driving, lifting and follow-up.

  • Hospital stayPlanned laparoscopic gallbladder surgery usually means going home the next day.
  • WalkingMost patients sit up and walk a few hours after waking from anaesthesia.
  • FoodLight liquids on the evening of surgery, soft low-fat food (dal, khichdi, boiled rice) for the first one to two weeks, then a normal diet.
  • WorkLight desk work and daily activities after about 5–7 days; avoid heavy lifting for 4–6 weeks.

Informed decisions

Benefits and risks belong in the same conversation

Discuss bleeding, infection, blood clots, bile leak, injury to the bile duct or nearby organs, retained stones and persistent digestive symptoms. The benefits and risks depend on your health and findings; a small-incision operation is still surgery.

Stomach pain after eating fatty food — what is it?

Pain in the upper right abdomen that starts or worsens after oily or heavy food is a common symptom of gallbladder stones; it can spread to the back or right shoulder. Acidity and other conditions can feel similar, so an ultrasound is needed to confirm the cause.

Can gallstones be treated without surgery?

Medicines and diet changes may ease symptoms for a while but do not reliably dissolve or remove gallbladder stones. Once stones cause symptoms, removing the gallbladder is the standard treatment to prevent complications. Stones found by chance without symptoms often do not need an operation.

Is gallbladder surgery risky?

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy is one of the most commonly performed operations. Like any operation it has risks, including bleeding, infection, bile leak and, rarely, bile-duct injury. Dr. Bharti explains the benefits and risks for your situation before you decide.

How long is the hospital stay after gallbladder surgery?

After planned laparoscopic gallbladder removal, most patients go home the next day. Inflamed or complicated gallbladders may need a longer stay.

What to eat after gallbladder surgery?

Start with light, low-fat food such as dal, khichdi and boiled rice for the first one to two weeks, then return gradually to a normal balanced diet. The liver continues to make bile, so most people have no long-term diet restriction.

What is a difficult gallbladder or acute cholecystitis?

Acute cholecystitis is inflammation of the gallbladder, often because a stone blocks its outlet. It causes constant pain, fever and tenderness and can make surgery more complex. It needs prompt hospital assessment, and early laparoscopic surgery is often advised.

Can gallbladder stones come back after surgery?

Stones cannot form again in the gallbladder because it is removed. Rarely, stones can form or be found later in the bile duct, which is treated separately.

What is the difference between open and laparoscopic cholecystectomy?

Open surgery uses one larger cut. Laparoscopic (keyhole) surgery uses three or four small cuts of 5–10 mm for a camera and instruments, usually with less pain, smaller scars and a quicker recovery. Occasionally a keyhole operation is converted to open surgery for safety.

Where is gallbladder pain felt?

Usually in the upper right abdomen or the upper middle abdomen below the ribs, sometimes spreading to the back or right shoulder blade. Similar pain has other causes, so an examination and ultrasound are needed.

Can gallbladder stones dissolve with medicines?

Medicines rarely clear gallbladder stones reliably, and stones often return when they are stopped. If stones are causing symptoms, discuss whether removing the gallbladder is appropriate.
