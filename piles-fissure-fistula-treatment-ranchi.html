Understanding Piles, Fissure and Fistula

These three anorectal conditions are often confused with each other. Each has distinct symptoms and requires a proper clinical evaluation before any treatment decision is made.

Piles (Haemorrhoids)Bleeding, discomfort, prolapseSwollen blood vessels in/around anus

Piles (Haemorrhoids)

Piles are enlarged blood vessels in and around the anal canal. They are extremely common. Bleeding during or after bowel movements is the most frequent symptom — bright red blood on tissue paper or in the toilet pan.

Symptoms

  • Bright red bleeding during or after passing stools
  • Itching or discomfort around the anus
  • Pain, especially with grade 3 or 4 prolapsed piles
  • A lump near the anus that may need to be pushed back in

Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the grade of piles. Early stages may be managed with dietary modification, fibre supplementation and sitz baths. Surgical treatment — Open Haemorrhoidectomy — is considered for grade 3 and grade 4 piles, or when conservative measures have not provided adequate relief, after proper evaluation.

Anal Fissure

An anal fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anal canal. It causes sharp, tearing pain during and after bowel movements, often described as passing broken glass. Bleeding may also occur.

Symptoms

  • Severe pain during and after passing stools — lasting minutes to hours
  • Small amount of bright red blood on tissue
  • A visible crack or tear at the anal opening
  • Spasm of the anal sphincter

Treatment Options

Acute fissures often respond to dietary changes, topical agents and sitz baths. Chronic fissures that do not respond to conservative management may require Lateral Internal Sphincterotomy (LIS) — a targeted procedure to relieve sphincter spasm and allow the fissure to heal — after evaluation by the surgeon.

Anal Fistula

An anal fistula is an abnormal tunnel connecting the inside of the anal canal to the skin outside. It develops most commonly after an anal abscess. It causes persistent discharge, recurrent infections and discomfort near the anal area.

Symptoms

  • Persistent discharge of pus or blood-stained fluid near the anus
  • Recurrent swelling and pain near the anal area
  • A small opening visible on the skin near the anus
  • Itching and discomfort in the perianal region

Treatment

Anal fistula cannot heal without surgical intervention. Fistulectomy — complete surgical excision of the fistula tract — is performed with careful attention to sphincter preservation. The exact technique depends on the type and complexity of the fistula, assessed during clinical examination and imaging.

What is the difference between piles, fissure and fistula?+

Piles are swollen blood vessels causing bleeding and discomfort. Fissure is a tear in the anal lining causing severe pain during bowel movements. Fistula is an abnormal tunnel from inside the anal canal to the skin outside, causing persistent discharge. All three are distinct conditions requiring proper evaluation before treatment.

Is surgery always needed for piles?+

Not always. Early-stage piles may respond to dietary changes, fibre supplementation and sitz baths. Surgical treatment is considered for grade 3 and grade 4 piles, or when conservative treatment has failed, after a proper clinical evaluation by Dr. Bharti.

Can fistula heal on its own without surgery?+

No. Anal fistula is an abnormal channel that cannot close spontaneously. Without surgical treatment, it causes repeated infections and abscesses. Fistulectomy, performed after proper evaluation, is the standard approach.
